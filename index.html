Key Finding — Grey Shade Problem
Grey shade family was identified as the primary cause of shade variation across Dec 2023 – Apr 2024. Dye molecules in grey recipes (3-dye combinations: Yellow S3R + Ruby S3B + Deep Blue) showed highest incompatibility and migration rates, directly impacting RFT.

Why Roller Hardness Matters — Key Insight
Squeezing of fabric depends directly on roller hardness. If hardness deviates below 60 or above 70 Shore units, the squeeze ratio changes → wet pick-up % varies → dye concentration absorbed per unit fabric changes → shade variation occurs in finished goods. CPB-2 showed the most out-of-range readings (3 occurrences below 60), making it the priority machine for maintenance scheduling.

7-Step Experiment Protocol
1
20ml water in small beaker
2
0.5gm dye dissolved in water
3
Set filter paper or plain fabric as medium
4
Mark centre point of filter paper precisely
5
Pour dye drop at centre of paper
6
Add water drop when previous dye absorbed & spread properly
7
When dye stops migrating, water migration also stops → measure ring diameter
Result
Grey Eclipse dyes (High concentration: 3.25–5.55g per 0.5L) showed significantly larger migration rings than Amber Sun dyes (Low concentration: 0.107–0.159g). High-concentration dyes = incompatible migration rates = shade ring formation in fabric.

Conclusion — Lead Cloth is the Primary Driver of Shade Variation
CPB route shows lead cloth pick-up % averaging 21–25% while batch pick-up % reaches 80–100%. This massive mismatch means the lead cloth does NOT represent the actual dyebath equilibrium — causing the wrong shade to be set before the bulk fabric enters the machine. Solution implemented: Lead cloth must be made from the same batch type and GSM as the production batch. This directly eliminates 15–20 slits of colour variation per batch.
